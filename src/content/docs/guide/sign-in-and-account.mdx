These screens sit outside the console frame: a single card on a plain page, with the instance's name
and logo above it. The console's own account screen is **Settings** > **Account**, which the avatar
menu's **Profile** opens. On a self-hosted instance with its own branding, the name you see is the
operator's, not Cloudgate's.

## What you can do

- Create an account on the Hosted instance, or claim a new self-hosted instance as its owner.
- Sign in with a password, two-factor or single sign-on, and choose an Organization.
- Reset a forgotten password by email.
- Accept an invitation to an Organization, with a new account or an existing one.
- Approve the Cloudgate CLI's login from the browser.
- Change your name, email, avatar and password; turn two-factor on or off; sign out other
  sessions; and create [personal keys](/docs/guide/personal-keys).

## Create an account

The sign-up screen, `/sign-up`, changes with the instance and how you arrived.

| Where you are | Heading | Button | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hosted instance | **Create your account** | **Create account** | You confirm your email, then you are signed in with an Organization of your own |
| Hosted, from a template's Deploy button | **Create your free account to deploy your app** | **Create account** | The same, and you land back in the [get-started wizard](/docs/guide/get-started) |
| A new self-hosted instance | **Set up this instance** | **Create owner account** | You own the instance; see below |
| From an invitation | **Create your account** | **Create account and join** | You join the inviting Organization |

### Fields

| Field | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **First name**, **Last name** | Your name, shown wherever Cloudgate names you | Both required |
| **Organization name** | Names the Organization made for you | Defaults to `My Organization`; rename it later in Settings. Not asked when you are invited |
| **Email** | What you sign in with | Filled in from an invitation |
| **Password** | Your password | At least 8 characters, at most 128 |
| **Confirm password** | The same again | Must match |

On the Hosted instance a short check may appear above the button to stop bots; complete it, then
press **Create account**.

### Confirm your email (Hosted)

After **Create account**, **Check your inbox** says a link went to your address. Open it to confirm
the address; you are then signed in. Until then the account cannot be used. **Send the link again**
sends a fresh one (one per address every few minutes), and **Go to sign in** returns to sign-in.

<Callout type="note" title="Unconfirmed accounts">
On the Hosted instance an account whose email is never confirmed is removed after seven days, so the
address can sign up again.
</Callout>

### Claim a new self-hosted instance

The first person to sign up on a self-hosted instance owns it. Open `http://<host>:3000/sign-up`
after the [install](/docs/self-host/install); the screen says **Set up this instance**, "The first
account owns this instance. Later ones join by invitation." Press **Create owner account**. No email
is sent and no confirmation is needed: you are signed in at once, in the Organization you named.

On a self-hosted sign-in screen nobody has claimed yet, the footer reads **New instance? Set it up**.

### When sign-up is refused

| Screen | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Already set up**: "This instance is closed to new sign-ups" | The self-hosted instance already has an owner | Ask an owner to [invite you](/docs/guide/members-and-roles), then use the invitation's link |
| **Not open yet**: "Sign-up is not open yet" | The Hosted instance is not taking accounts yet | Come back later |
| "An account already uses that email address" | You already have an account | Sign in instead, or use another address |

## Sign in

Open the console's address, or `/sign-in`. Enter your **Email** and **Password** and press **Sign in**.
If you arrived from a template's Deploy button, the heading is **Sign in to deploy your app**.

- **Forgot your password?** opens the reset; see below.
- **No account yet? Create one** shows on the Hosted instance; a self-hosted instance that is
  already claimed shows no sign-up link.
- After too many attempts, **Sign in** stays off until the wait the message gives is over.

What happens next depends on your account:

1. With two-factor on, you are asked for a code (below).
2. If you belong to one Organization, or Cloudgate already knows which one to open, you land on its
   [Organization home](/docs/guide/console-tour#organization-home).
3. If you belong to several and Cloudgate cannot tell which to open, **Choose an Organization**
   lists them; pick one. You can switch later from the masthead.
4. If you belong to none, **No organization yet** says so. Open the link in an invitation to join
   one, then sign in again, or **Sign out**.

<Screenshot name="sign-in" alt="The Cloudgate sign-in card with Email and Password fields, a Forgot your password link and the Sign in button, then or, a Work email field and Continue with SSO" caption="On an instance with single sign-on set up, the card adds a work email field." />

### Sign in with SSO

When your Organization signs in through an identity provider, the sign-in card has **or** and a
**Work email** field under the password form. Type your work address and press **Continue with
SSO**; Cloudgate reads the domain to find your identity provider. When SSO is enforced, the card
has only the work email and **Continue with SSO**. Setting SSO up is on
[SSO, SCIM and forward auth](/docs/guide/sso-and-scim).

## Two-step verification at sign-in

With two-factor on, sign-in continues to **Two-step verification** (`/2fa`). Choose the method at the
top:

| Method | What to type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Authenticator app** | The 6-digit code from your app | The code changes every 30 seconds |
| **Backup code** | One of the backup codes you saved | Each backup code works once |

Press **Verify**. **Back to sign in** starts over.

| What you see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **This attempt has expired** | The code prompt does not stay open for long | **Back to sign in**, and sign in again |
| **Too many wrong codes**, "This sign-in attempt is closed" | Too many wrong codes in one attempt | Sign in again to start a new attempt |
| **Account locked**, "Too many wrong codes" | Repeated wrong codes | The account is locked for 15 minutes; wait, then sign in again |
| **Nothing to verify** | You opened `/2fa` with no sign-in waiting on a code | **Go to sign in** |

<Callout type="limit" title="Lost both the app and the backup codes">
The sign-in screen cannot turn two-factor off. Only whoever manages the platform host, the machine
Cloudgate runs on, can turn it off for your account. On the Hosted instance, email
`support@octabyte.io`.
</Callout>

## Reset a forgotten password

<Steps>
<Step title="Ask for a link">
On sign-in, press **Forgot your password?**. On **Reset your password**, enter your **Email** and press
**Email me a link**. **Check your email** says the link is on its way if there is an account for that
address; it does not say whether there is one.
</Step>
<Step title="Open the link">
The link is good for one hour and works once. It opens **Choose a new password**.
</Step>
<Step title="Set the new password">
Enter a **New password** (at least 8 characters) and **Confirm new password**, then press **Set new
password**. **Password changed** confirms it; press **Sign in**.
</Step>
</Steps>

| What you see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Link is no longer valid** | The link was used, or more than an hour passed | **Request a new link**, and use the newest email |
| **Link is incomplete** | Part of the address is missing | Copy the whole link, or request a new one |
| **Reset is not set up**: "This instance cannot send email yet" | The instance has no email sender | Ask whoever runs the instance to configure one; see [I forgot my password and no email arrives](/docs/help/troubleshooting#self-hosted-i-forgot-my-password-and-no-email-arrives) |

## Accept an invitation

An invitation email links to `/accept-invitation/<id>`. An invitation is for one email address.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the link">
Signed out, **You have been invited** says who was invited to which Organization. Press **Sign in to
accept** if you have an account with that address, or **Create an account** to make one; the sign-up
form fills in the invited address and skips the Organization name.
</Step>
<Step title="Accept">
Signed in, **Join this Organization** shows the **Organization**, **Your role** and the **Invited
address**. Press **Accept invitation**. **You are in** confirms it; press **Open the console**.
</Step>
</Steps>

| Screen | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Wrong account** | You are signed in with another address. Press **Sign out**, then sign in as the invited person |
| **Invitation expired** | It was not accepted in time; ask for a new one |
| **Invitation declined** | You declined it; a declined invitation cannot be accepted, so ask for a new one |
| **Invitation withdrawn** | Whoever sent it called it back |
| **Already accepted** | You used it already; **Open the console** lets you in, unless you were removed since |
| **Invitation not found** or **Invitation not valid** | It was withdrawn, used, removed, or the link is incomplete |

Sending invitations is covered on [Members and roles](/docs/guide/members-and-roles).

## Log in the Cloudgate CLI

`cloudgate login` prints a code and tells you to open your console's address with `/device` added,
for example `https://cloudgate.octabyte.app/device`. If you are signed out there, you sign in first
and come back to the same page.

<Steps>
<Step title="Enter the code">
**Log in the Cloudgate CLI** asks for the **Code** your terminal shows. Spaces and dashes do not
matter. Press **Continue**.
</Step>
<Step title="Check and approve">
The code is shown large, like `WXYZ-2345`. Approve only if it is the code your own terminal is
showing. **Approve** gives that terminal a personal key in your name, ending in 90 days. **Deny**
gives it nothing.
</Step>
<Step title="Go back to the terminal">
**Your terminal is logged in**: the command carries on by itself. The key is listed under
[Personal keys](/docs/guide/personal-keys), where you can revoke it.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot name="device-approval" alt="The Log in the Cloudgate CLI card showing the code VFMS-8XBT with Approve and Deny buttons" caption="Approve only a code your own terminal is showing." />

A code lasts 30 minutes. An unknown or expired code says "That code is not one this Instance knows,
or it has expired"; run `cloudgate login` again. See [`cloudgate login`](/docs/cli/commands#cloudgate-login).

## Manage your account

**Settings** > **Account** (`/settings/account`, which `/account` also opens) holds five cards.

### Profile

| Field | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Your first name | Required |
| **Last name** | Shown after your name wherever Cloudgate names you | Optional |
| **Email** | What you sign in with | Saved in lower case |
| **Current password** | Appears when you change the email | Required to change the email |
| **Avatar** | The address of an image | Optional. Cloudgate has nowhere to upload one, so the image must already be online |

Press **Save changes**. On the Hosted instance a new email gets a link, and you keep signing in with
the old address until you open it. On a self-hosted instance the new address works at once, and
nothing is sent to either address.

### Password

Enter your **Current password**, a **New password** (at least 8 characters) and **New password again**,
then press **Change password**. **Sign out every other session** is on by default: everywhere else this
account is signed in stops working, and this session keeps going.

### Two-factor

The card shows **On** or **Off**.

- **Turn on** opens **Turn on two-factor** in two steps. First **Confirm it is you**: type your
  password and press **Continue**. Then **Scan, save and confirm**: scan the QR code with an
  authenticator app (or copy the setup key into it), save the ten backup codes with the copy button,
  type **The six-digit code from the app** and press **Turn on two-factor**. Two-factor is not on
  until that code is accepted.
- **Turn off** opens **Turn off two-factor**: type your password and press **Turn off two-factor**, or
  **Keep it on**.

<Screenshot name="account-two-factor" alt="The Turn on two-factor dialog with a QR code, the setup key, ten backup codes in a warning box and a field for the six-digit code" caption="The backup codes are shown only in this step." />

<Callout type="warn" title="Backup codes are shown once">
Closing the dialog does not bring them back. Turning two-factor off deletes the authenticator secret
and every remaining backup code; turning it on again means a new scan and a new set of codes.
</Callout>

### Sessions

The **Sessions** card lists every place your account is signed in, with its **Device**,
**Address**, **Started** and **Expires**. **Device** is the browser's User-Agent string exactly as
it was sent, such as `Mozilla/5.0 (X11; Ubuntu; Linux x86_64; rv:133.0) Gecko/20100101 Firefox/133.0`;
Cloudgate does not turn it into a browser name. Hover a cut-off string to read it whole. Your
current one is marked **This session**.

- **Sign out** on **This session** signs you out here. It is the only button on that row.
- **Revoke** on any other row signs that session out at once: "That session is signed out."

A session lasts three days and is renewed each day you use it. In the rare case the card says **This
list needs a session less than a day old**, press **Sign out** and sign in again to read it. You do not need that
to end other sessions: change your password with **Sign out every other session** on.

### Personal keys

Keys that let a script, CI job or the CLI act as you. See [Personal keys](/docs/guide/personal-keys).

<Screenshot name="account-sessions" alt="The Sessions card listing two raw User-Agent strings: this session, marked This session with Sign out, and a Firefox session on Ubuntu with Revoke, each with its address, start and expiry" caption="Revoke signs another session out immediately." />

## What you cannot do

<Callout type="limit" title="Limits">
- A self-hosted instance takes one owner sign-up; everyone after joins by invitation.
- You cannot upload an avatar, only link one.
- You cannot turn two-factor off from the sign-in screen, or reset someone else's password or
  two-factor from the console.
- Password reset and email confirmation need the instance to send email.
- An invitation works only for the address it was sent to.
</Callout>
