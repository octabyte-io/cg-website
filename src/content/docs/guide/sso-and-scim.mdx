<Callout type="limit" title="Paid features">
Single sign-on, SCIM and forward auth need the Business plan on the Hosted instance, or an
Enterprise license on a self-hosted instance. Setting them up takes an owner or an admin.
**Require single sign-on** needs more: an owner or admin of the instance's own Organization.
</Callout>

## What you can do

- Sign members in through an OIDC identity provider for your email domains.
- On the Hosted instance, prove the email domains your providers may sign in.
- Provision and deactivate members from your directory with SCIM.
- Put an Application's Domain behind a sign-in at your identity provider, with forward auth.
- On a self-hosted instance, turn off password sign-in for everyone.

## The Single sign-on screen

**Settings** > **Single sign-on** (`/settings/sso`) has, top to bottom: **Identity providers**,
**Proven domains** (Hosted only), **How people sign in**, **Trusted origins** and **Directory
provisioning (SCIM)**.

<Screenshot name="sso" alt="Settings, Single sign-on for Northwind Labs on a self-hosted instance, before any identity provider is added, with How people sign in and Trusted origins below" caption="Identity providers, sign-in, trusted origins and SCIM on one screen." />

Without the plan or license, the screen shows one card that says what single sign-on and SCIM are,
with **Upgrade to Business**, **Start 30-day free trial** or **Subscribe** on the Hosted instance
(for an owner or admin), or **Go to License** on a self-hosted instance.

## Set up single sign-on

<Steps>
<Step title="Prove your email domain (Hosted)">
Under **Proven domains**, type the part of your people's addresses after the @, such as
`northwind.example`, in **Add a domain** and press **Add domain**. The row reads **Waiting for the
record** and shows the TXT record's **Type**, **Name** and **Value**, each with a copy button.
Publish it at your DNS provider, then press **Check now**. Nothing checks by itself, so press it
again once the record is live, until the row reads **Proven**.

</Step>
<Step title="Add the provider">
Under **Identity providers**, press **Add a provider**. Fill in **Add an OIDC provider**:

| Field | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Provider id** | A short name for this connection; it appears in the sign-in URL and the redirect URI. | Lower-case letters, numbers and dashes, unique on the whole instance. Cannot be changed later. |
| **Issuer URL** | Your provider's base URL, such as `https://login.northwind.example`. | Cloudgate reads its discovery document, and fetches it only from a trusted origin. |
| **Trust this issuer** | Adds the issuer's origin to **Trusted origins** for you. | Ticked by default. Untick it to add the origin yourself first. |
| **Email domains** | The domains this provider signs in, comma separated: `northwind.example`, not a URL. | Required. A domain another provider on the instance already claims is refused. |
| **Client id**, **Client secret** | From the application you registered at your provider. | The secret is stored and never shown again. |
| **Authorization endpoint**, **Token endpoint**, **User info endpoint**, **JWKS endpoint** | Only if your provider needs them. | Optional. Left empty, they come from `<issuer>/.well-known/openid-configuration`. |
| **Scopes** | Comma separated, such as `openid, email, profile`. | Optional. Left empty, the provider's defaults apply. |
</Step>
<Step title="Register the redirect URI">
As soon as you type the **Provider id**, the dialog shows the **Redirect URI** with a copy button.
Add it to your identity provider's allowed list, or sign-ins are refused at the last step. Then
press **Add the provider**.
</Step>
</Steps>

People whose address is on the provider's domains now sign in through it instead of with a
password. Someone who arrives through SSO joins only that provider's Organization.

### Edit or remove a provider

Each provider shows its **Provider id**, an **OIDC** or **SAML** chip, **Secret stored** when a
client secret is saved, its issuer, the domains it signs in and its **Redirect URI**.

- **Edit** opens the same form. The **Provider id** is fixed. To keep the stored client
  configuration, leave the secret blank and change nothing under **OIDC client**; change any of it
  and you must type the **Client secret** again. Moving to a different issuer needs its origin in
  **Trusted origins** first.
- **Remove** asks "Remove ...?". Nobody on its domains can sign in through it any more. Accounts
  made through it, and their memberships, stay. A forward-auth proxy deployed against it stops
  authenticating. Press **Remove the provider**.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not remove the only provider while SSO is required">
If **Require single sign-on** is on and this is the only provider, removing it leaves nobody a way
in, including you.
</Callout>

## Trusted origins

**Trusted origins** lists the hosts Cloudgate may fetch a provider's configuration from, and the
origins a browser may sign in from. No identity provider can be reached until its origin is listed.
Type an origin (scheme and host, such as `https://login.northwind.example`) in **Add an origin**
and press **Trust it**. The cross beside an origin stops trusting it. On the Hosted instance it
must be a public address.

## Require single sign-on

**How people sign in** has one switch, **Require single sign-on**. It is instance-wide: it applies
to every Organization on this deployment, not only yours. When it is on, the sign-in screen does
not show a password form at all.

- It is off until at least one provider is registered.
- Turning it on asks "Turn off password sign-in for everyone?". Before you press **Require single
  sign-on**, sign in through your provider in another browser and confirm it works.
- Only an owner or admin of the instance's own Organization (the first one made on a self-hosted
  instance) can switch it. On the Hosted instance that is Octabyte, so customers cannot turn it on.

<Callout type="warn" title="There is no way back from the console">
If nobody can sign in through a provider once passwords are off, nothing in the console can turn
it off again: the way back is a change in the database.
</Callout>

## Provision members with SCIM

<Steps>
<Step title="Add a SCIM provider">
Under **Directory provisioning (SCIM)**, press **Add a provider**. In **Add a SCIM provider**, type
a **Provider id**: lower-case letters, numbers and dashes, unique on the whole instance. It cannot
be changed: it becomes part of the token and of every account it creates. Have somewhere to paste
the token, then press **Create the token**.
</Step>
<Step title="Copy the base URL and token">
"... is connected" shows the **Bearer token**, the **SCIM base URL** (the console's address plus
`/api/auth/scim/v2`) and the **Provider id**. Paste the URL and token into your directory; it sends
the token as `Authorization: Bearer ...`. Press **I have copied it**.

<Screenshot name="sso-scim-token" alt="The okta-scim is connected dialog showing a bearer token, the SCIM base URL and the provider id" caption="This is the only time the token is shown." />
</Step>
</Steps>

Your directory now creates, updates and deactivates members. A member deactivated over SCIM is
marked **deactivated** on the Members screen and cannot sign in; only the directory can reactivate
them.

Each SCIM provider row says its token was shown once, when it was created, and, if your
directory tried to add someone while the Organization was below the paid features, **Last refused
add** with the time and address. **Remove** asks "Remove ...?": the token stops working at once
and your directory starts reporting failures. Members it created stay as they are. Press **Remove
the provider**.

## Put an Application behind forward auth

Forward auth runs an authentication proxy (oauth2-proxy) on a Server's Traefik, so visitors to a
Domain must sign in at your identity provider before they reach the Application. It uses an OIDC
provider you added here: set an authentication domain under **Settings** > **Forward auth**,
deploy the proxy against the provider, then turn on the sign-in switch beside a host on the
Application's **Domains** tab. Every step is in [Forward auth](/docs/guide/forward-auth).

<Callout type="warn" title="Removing the proxy breaks gated Domains">
After **Remove the proxy**, every Domain whose sign-in switch is on fails every request until the
proxy is deployed again or its switch is turned off.
</Callout>

## What happens when the plan or license lapses?

The settings are kept, and work again on Business or once a license is active:

- A strip at the top says what is off, such as "SSO sign-in is off, and SCIM can't add people."
- Providers are marked **Off** with where, such as "Off on Starter". **Add a provider**, **Edit**,
  **Add domain**, **Check now** and **Trust it** are off; **Remove** still works.
- SCIM providers show **Removing only**: your directory can still deactivate people but not add them.
- SSO-only people use **Forgot password** to set a password.
- Forward auth keeps protecting gated Domains (**Still protected**) but cannot be added or changed.

On the Hosted instance, after **Move to Starter** is scheduled, a strip names the date these stop,
with **Keep Business**.

## What you cannot do

<Callout type="limit" title="Limits">
- SAML providers can be listed and removed, but not added or edited in the console.
- On the Hosted instance, a provider signs in only addresses in a proven domain, and its issuer,
  endpoints and trusted origins must be public addresses.
- A Provider id, for SSO or SCIM, cannot be changed after it is made.
- A SCIM token is shown once; to replace it, remove the provider and add a new one.
</Callout>
