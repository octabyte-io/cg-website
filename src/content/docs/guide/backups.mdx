The examples back up the PostgreSQL database `pg-main` and the `uploads` volume of the `web`
Application in `storefront` to a Destination named `Nightly backups`.

## What you can do

- Add S3-compatible buckets as **Destinations** for the whole Organization.
- Schedule database dumps, keep the newest few, run one now, and restore one.
- Schedule volume backups of an Application's or a bucket's volumes, and restore a tarball.
- Set how long AWS or Google Cloud keeps a cloud database's automated backups.

## Add a Destination

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Destinations">
Open **Settings** > **Destinations** and press **Add a destination**.
<Screenshot name="settings-destinations" alt="The Destinations settings page listing a backup bucket with its name, bucket, endpoint and region" caption="Destinations are shared by every Project in the Organization." />
</Step>
<Step title="Describe the bucket">
Fill in **Name** (only you see it), **Endpoint** (with its scheme), **Region** (empty if your
provider has none), **Bucket**, **Provider**, **Access key** and **Secret access key**. The secret
is stored encrypted and never shown again.
</Step>
<Step title="Test and save">
Press **Test connection**, then save. Cloudgate reaches the bucket with rclone, so anything
S3-compatible works.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="Keep backups off the machine">
A Destination on another provider or region survives losing the Server the data is on. That is the
point of writing backups off it.
</Callout>

## Schedule database dumps

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the Backups tab">
Open the database, such as `pg-main`, and its **Backups** tab.
</Step>
<Step title="Add a schedule">
Under **Scheduled dumps** press **Add a schedule** and set:

- **Database**: the database inside this engine to dump.
- **Destination**: where the dumps are written.
- **Prefix**: the path in the bucket.
- **Cron expression**: five fields, such as `0 2 * * *` for every day at 02:00.
- **Keep the newest**: older dumps under the prefix are deleted after each successful run. Empty
  keeps every one.
- **Run on the cron**: off keeps the schedule without firing it.
<Screenshot name="database-backups" alt="The Backups tab of pg-main, with one scheduled dump and the dumps found in the destination" caption="The list below the schedules is read straight from the bucket." />
</Step>
<Step title="Run it once">
Press **Run now** on the row. **Pause** and **Resume** stop and restart the cron. Every run is
listed on the Environment's **Deployments** tab as a database backup with its log.
</Step>
</Steps>

Engines dump differently: MySQL dumps run as root, and a libSQL backup is a tar of its data
directory. Redis has no dump, so its **Backups** tab says Cloudgate cannot back it up.

## Restore a database dump

<Callout type="warn" title="A restore replaces what is in the database now">
Restoring writes the dump over the database's current contents, with no copy of what was there.
Take a fresh dump first if you might need it.
</Callout>

<Steps>
<Step title="Find the dump">
On the **Backups** tab, under **Dumps in the destination**, choose the Destination and press
**Restore** on the file.
</Step>
<Step title="Name the database">
The dialog's steps are **Choose the dump**, **Name the database** and **Replace its contents**.
Name the database inside the engine that the dump goes into.
</Step>
<Step title="Confirm">
Type the database's name to confirm and restore.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Back up a volume

Volume backups copy a named Docker volume off its Server as a tarball.

<Steps>
<Step title="Open Volume backups">
Open the Application, such as `web`, and its **Volume backups** tab. For a bucket on a Server, use
the volume backups on the bucket's own screen.
</Step>
<Step title="Add a backup">
Press **Add a backup** and set **Name**, **Volume**, **Destination**, **Prefix**,
**Cron expression** and **Keep the newest**.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide whether to stop the Service">
Tick **Stop the service while the backup runs** for a volume that is written to all the time, such
as a database's. A volume tarred while being written can produce a tarball that will not restore;
stopping costs downtime and is the only way to be certain.
</Step>
<Step title="Run it now">
Press **Run now** on the row to check it. The outcome lands in the run history below.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Restore a volume

<Steps>
<Step title="Stop what uses the volume">
A volume cannot be restored while any container, running or exited, references it. **Stop** the
Application; for a Docker Compose stack, use **Remove containers**.
</Step>
<Step title="Start the restore">
On the **Volume backups** tab, press **Restore a volume** under **Restore**, or **Restore** on a
backup row.
</Step>
<Step title="Choose and confirm">
Go through **Choose the tarball**, **Confirm the volume** and **Replace its contents**. The
tarball is unpacked over the volume on the Server.
</Step>
<Step title="Start it again">
**Start** the Application, or the stack.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warn" title="The volume's current contents are replaced">
A volume restore keeps no copy of what was in the volume. Only tarballs still in the Destination
are offered: a backup that keeps the newest five has already deleted the rest.
</Callout>

## Backups of databases in AWS and Google Cloud

A database in a Cloud account has no dump schedules: "Dump backups to a Destination are
Server-only for now." Its **Backups** tab shows what the cloud keeps, read live: the retention
and, where the cloud reports one, the earliest and latest restorable time.

| Database | Card | Range |
|---|---|---|
| RDS (PostgreSQL, MySQL) | **Backup retention**, in days | 1 to 35; it cannot be turned off here |
| ElastiCache (Redis) | **Snapshot retention**, in days | 0 to 35; 0 turns snapshots off |
| Cloud SQL (PostgreSQL, MySQL) | **Backup retention**, daily backups kept | 1 to 365; point-in-time recovery is on beside them |
| Memorystore (Redis) | **Backup retention**, in days | 0 to 365; 0 turns backups off |

To restore, use the cloud's own console, linked from the tab. A restore there makes a new instance.
Deleting a cloud database takes a final snapshot unless you ask to delete its data too.

## Who can do what

| Action | Who |
|---|---|
| Add, test, delete a Destination | Owner or admin |
| Schedule, run, pause and restore dumps and volume backups | Members granted the Service, owners and admins |

Two backups or restores of the same volume never run at once: the second is refused with a 409
naming the busy volume, and a scheduled backup waits its turn.

## What you cannot do

- Dump Redis, or schedule dumps for a database in a Cloud account.
- Restore a cloud database from the Cloudgate console.
- Restore a volume while a container still references it.
- Schedule volume backups from a Compose stack's own screen: the **Volume backups** tab is on
  Applications and on buckets.
- Count on a Deployment to back anything up.
