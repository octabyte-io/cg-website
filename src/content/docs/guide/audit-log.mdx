Owners and admins read the audit log under **Settings** > **Audit log** (`/settings/audit-log`).
It lists, newest first, every create, change, delete and deploy a signed-in member made through the
console or the API, every sign-in and sign-out, and every call an Agent made, whether it
succeeded, was refused or failed.

## What you can do

- Search by who did it and by the name of what it was done to.
- Filter by actor, action, kind, outcome and a date range.
- Open an entry to see the role the actor held at the time, where the request came from and what
  else it recorded.
- See everything one Agent did, from a link on its row in Settings > **Agents**.

## The audit log screen

<Screenshot name="audit-log" alt="Settings, Audit log for Northwind Labs with its filters and the newest entries: schedules and users created and domains updated by ada@northwind.example" caption="Newest first; each row has Details." />

A line above the table counts the entries, for example "1,204 entries recorded. A person's reads,
connection tests and refused requests are not recorded." With a filter on, it says how many match.

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **When** | When it happened. |
| **Who** | The person's name or email, an Agent (marked **Agent**), or **Cloudgate** (marked **Platform**) for actions Cloudgate took itself. Under it, their role at the time. |
| **Action** | What was done, such as `create`, `deploy` or `rollback`. |
| **Outcome** | **Succeeded**, **Refused** or **Failed**. |
| **What** | The name of what it was done to, and its kind, such as `domain` or `application`. |
| **Details** | Opens the entry. |

The table shows 25 entries at a time, with previous and next buttons below it.

## Filter the audit log

| Filter | What it matches | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Email contains...** | Any part of the actor's email address. | Search box. |
| **Name contains...** | Any part of the name of what it was done to. | Search box. |
| **Actor** | **Anyone**, or **The platform** for Cloudgate's own actions. | |
| **Action** | One action exactly: `create`, `update`, `delete`, `deploy`, `cancel`, `redeploy`, `rollback`, `login`, `logout`, `restore`, `run`, `start`, `stop`, `restart`, `kill`, `reload`, `rebuild` or `read`. | **Any action** clears it. |
| **Kind** | One kind of thing exactly, such as `application`, `database`, `domain`, `server`, `customRole`, `sso`, `scim`, `billing` or `session`. | **Any kind** clears it. |
| **Outcome** | **Succeeded**, **Refused** or **Failed**. | Kept in the page address (`?outcome=refused`), so you can share the link. |
| **From**, **To** | A date and time range. | |

**Clear filters** resets them all. On a narrow screen the filters fold behind a **Filters** button
that counts how many are set. If nothing matches, the table says **Nothing matches those filters**:
try a wider date range or clear a filter.

## Find who changed something

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the audit log">
Open **Settings** > **Audit log**.
</Step>
<Step title="Narrow it down">
Type part of the thing's name in **Name contains...**, such as `shop.northwind.example`, and
choose its **Kind**, such as `domain`. Add an **Action** or a date range if the list is long.
</Step>
<Step title="Open the entry">
Press **Details** on the row.
</Step>
</Steps>

## What an entry shows

The dialog is titled with the action and kind, such as "update · domain", and the name of what it
acted on.

<Screenshot name="audit-log-detail" alt="The details of an audit log entry: update of the domain shop.northwind.example by ada@northwind.example, owner, with the request's route under Recorded with it" caption="Every entry shows who, their role at the time, the outcome and what it acted on." />

| Row | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Who** | The actor. |
| **Their role at the time** | The role they held when it happened, even if it has changed since. |
| **Outcome** | Succeeded, refused or failed. |
| **When** | The exact time. |
| **Action**, **Kind** | What was done, to what kind of thing. |
| **Name**, **Id** | The name and id of what it was done to. |
| **From** | The IP address the request came from, **Not recorded**, or **No request** for something Cloudgate did itself. |

Below that, when the entry recorded more: a before-and-after view where it stored both values (a
member's role change does), a list of the values under **Recorded with it**, or the raw text under
**Recorded with it, as stored**. Most changes record who, what and when, without the old and new
values.

## See everything one Agent did

On an Agent's row in **Settings** > **Agents**, **Every call it made, in the audit log** opens the log filtered to that
Agent (the address carries `?userId=`). A line says "Showing only the entries of ..." with **Show
everyone** to go back. See [Agents](/docs/guide/agents).

## What is recorded

| Recorded | Not recorded |
|---|---|
| Creates, changes, deletes and deploys by members, through the console or the API | A person's reads |
| Sign-ins and sign-outs | Connection tests |
| Every Agent call, allowed, refused (with the reason) or failed | A person's refused requests |
| Actions Cloudgate takes itself, such as applying a Template's Plan for you | Calls through the Gateway (see Token spend on the AI model instead) |

## Entries from deleted Organizations

When an Organization is deleted, its entries are kept but no longer say which Organization they
came from. Only an owner or admin of the instance's own Organization can read them. For them, a
line says how many there are, with **Show them**; the list then shows only those entries, with
**Back to this Organization**. Everyone else sees a line that such entries are kept.

## Who can read the audit log?

Owners and admins, or a custom role given the audit log permission.

<Callout type="limit" title="Owners and admins">
Reading the log needs the audit log permission, which owners and admins hold and members do not. A
custom role can be given it (custom roles need the Business plan or an Enterprise license). The
log itself needs no plan or license. Without the permission, the screen says it cannot be read.
</Callout>

## What you cannot do

<Callout type="limit" title="Limits">
- Entries cannot be edited or removed, and there is no export button.
- Action and Kind match exactly, one value at a time.
- A person's reads and refused requests are not recorded.
</Callout>
